# Website Positioning and Messaging

- Current website reads as confused: mixes risk advisory, insurance brokerage, and employee benefits without a clear throughline
- Core positioning debate: narrow to employee benefits vs. broader business risk coverage for foreign subsidiaries
- Agreed direction: own the “foreign subsidiary in India” space without abandoning employee benefits
  - Foreign subsidiaries (IT, manufacturing, hospitality, GCCs) are the primary target
  - Employee benefits remains the flagship product
  - Broader business risk coverage (cyber, DNO, property, supply chain, compliance) sits alongside it
- Proposed storyline: understand risk, analyze, protect, manage, recover
  - Business risk has evolved beyond financial risk: cyber attacks, supply chain disruption, employee lawsuits, natural catastrophe
  - Messaging should lead with protection of the business, not just insurance products

# Content and Page Structure

- Homepage is too aggressive on “risk audit” and “B2B code” framing: remove or deprioritize
- Sections to clean up or remove:
  - Institutional risk desk, enterprise risk architecture headings: too jargon-heavy
  - Underwriter ratings and underwriter references: remove entirely
  - Industry verticals (aerospace, gaming): remove, not relevant
- Keep and strengthen:
  - Employee benefits as a clear, prominent section
  - Business insurance as a second pillar with the protection storyline
  - Resources/blog section for risk education content
- Productized packaging layout noted as a useful direction: low-friction, “pick and go” feel for US/American audiences

# CTA and Developer Involvement

- Current CTAs (risk audit, B2B quote) are too direct and sales-heavy: replace
  - Primary CTA: “Get in touch” or “Talk to an expert” (call, WhatsApp, or meeting)
  - Secondary CTA: AI chatbot for early-stage discovery visitors who aren’t ready to share contact details
  - Chatbot framing: “Chat to understand how to navigate the insurance landscape in India”
- Two visitor types to design for:
  - Already evaluating: needs a clear, coherent story to convert
  - Early discovery: needs a low-friction entry point (chatbot, not a form)
- Developer working on the project needs deeper business context, not just feedback loops
  - Should be involved in brainstorming sessions with Subham
  - Needs to understand the two-part positioning to make good decisions independently
